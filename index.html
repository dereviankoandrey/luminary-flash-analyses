The Solana Beach thesis is fundamentally different from every prior validated market: it's not about absorption velocity (too expensive for fast turnover) or regulatory scarcity (San Diego doesn't have Denver-level supply constraints). It's long-term appreciation of irreproducible coastal inventory + dual-income cash flow buffering insurance/tax drag.

Solana Beach has ZERO new construction within the current buildable footprint. Every property is 25+ years old. This creates absolute supply scarcity — a feature typically associated with Denver, but here it operates alongside Pacific West market appreciation rates (4-7% annually) that exceed Sun Belt averages.

Key condition: dual-income units are essential for insurance/tax coverage at this price point. Single-family rental underwriting does NOT work in San Diego unless the property generates $15K+/mo gross.

This is the only deal type that works at sub-$700K in San Diego County: East County (El Cajon, Santee, Lakeside) represents the lowest entry point for coastal-adjacent appreciation. 4-unit buildings provide natural income diversification while ADU conversion potential adds future value creation.

The critical California-specific advantage: ADU laws passed from 2017 onward allow virtually any residential lot with single-family zoning to add a unit of up to 1,200 SF — adding significant rental income and resale value. This regulatory tailwind DOES NOT EXIST in most Sun Belt markets (where ADUs face significant local approval hurdles).

Key condition: requires owner-occupancy for at least one year due to state financing rules, meaning a minimum 12-month hold with no exit option during that window.
