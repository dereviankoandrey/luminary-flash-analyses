The Thesis

Phoenix operates uniquely among Sun Belt markets. The Arizona legislature passed comprehensive landlord-friendly legislation while the state attracts 400+ net domestic migrants daily. Unlike Dallas (flat supply floor) or Miami (geographic limits), Phoenix has both buildable land AND water constraints — creating a scenario where short-term oversupply in specific suburban enclaves intersects with long-term structural appreciation from population growth.

The best playbook for Phoenix right now: Smaller multifamily (4-12 units) in established neighborhoods with confirmed water rights, buying older structures and executing cosmetic-to-moderate renovations at $8-12K/unit — well below the $15-18K national average due to lower Arizona labor costs.

Near-Term Risk: The Supply Bomb

⚠️ Critical signal: Phoenix issued 164% more building permits in the past 24 months than its prior five-year average. While older established neighborhoods aren't experiencing new construction, broad market appreciation pressure from overbuilding is compressing flips spreads by ~10-15% versus historical norms. This deal remains profitable — just with a lower ceiling.
