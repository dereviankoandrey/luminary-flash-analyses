? Frequently Asked Questions — AI Hallucination Detection

What is an AI hallucination in content?▾
An AI hallucination occurs when a language model generates factual claims, statistics, or citations that sound confident but are fabricated or unverifiable. In SEO context, publishing hallucinated content triggers Google's E-E-A-T (Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness) penalties, causing organic traffic to collapse.
How do I detect if AI-generated text contains hallucinations?▾
Use our free tool above: paste any passage and get real-time scoring across 5 dimensions — source citation quality (are claims backed by cited sources?), factual consistency (internal contradictions), confidence calibration (does the model claim more certainty than evidence supports), specificity score (real numbers vs. vague generalizations), and a composite hallucination risk index.
Why do AI-generated SEO articles get zero traffic?▾
Google increasingly penalizes content with hallucinated or unverifiable claims — the core of their E-E-A-T enforcement. When AI models generate plausible-sounding but false statistics, missing citations, or internally contradictory claims, search engines flag the content as low-trust and suppress rankings. This is exactly why a deterministic verification tool matters: it checks every claim before publication.
Is this tool free? Does it send my content to any server?▾
Yes, the tool is completely free and runs 100% in your browser using client-side JavaScript. No data you paste ever leaves your device — all analysis happens locally in memory. The email capture form (below results) uses a separate submission mechanism for waitlist purposes only.
How does Luminary's scoring methodology work?▾
Our engine evaluates text against known hallucination patterns: unverifiable statistical claims, internally contradictory data points, citation formatting anomalies (fake journal names, missing DOIs, fabricated author credentials), confidence calibration mismatches (high certainty language with weak supporting evidence), and vagueness patterns that replace real numbers on specific claims. Each dimension is scored 0-10 independently, then a weighted composite determines overall risk.
What types of content work best for this scan?▾
The tool performs best on structured, factual content: real estate analyses, financial reports, medical research summaries, legal briefs, marketing copy that makes specific claims, and any text where accuracy matters. It's less useful for creative writing or opinion pieces where there are no verifiable facts to check.
