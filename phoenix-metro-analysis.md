## EXECUTIVE THESIS

Phoenix operates under a **supply-frontier-under-demand-growth** regime that is structurally different from every other Sun Belt market we've analyzed. The Arizona legislature recently passed comprehensive landlord-friendly legislation while the state continues attracting 400+ net domestic migratees daily. What makes Phoenix unique: there's still buildable land within commuting distance to employment centers, but that supply has an environmental floor — groundwater — which creates a natural scarcity mechanism for the long term even while short-term builders are flooding specific submarkets with new construction.

**Best opportunistic play:** Smaller multifamily acquisitions (4-12 units) in established neighborhoods where water-rights infrastructure is confirmed and older-stock unit values create immediate value-add potential through renovation. The market pays a premium for turnkey operations because migration velocity means tenant demand resets monthly, but the highest ROI comes from buying distressed older structures in areas with secure groundwater allocations and executing cosmetic-to-moderate renovations at $8-12K/unit — well below national average of $15-18K/unit due to lower Arizona labor costs.

## THREE DEAL ARCHETYPES ANALYZED

### Deal 1: Fix-and-Flip in East Valley Mesa ($280-350K Acquisition + $45-60K Rehab)

**Property Type:** Single-family, build era 1970-1985, ~1,200 sqft on standard lot  
**Target Submarket:** Mesa/East Mesa — older established neighborhoods north of Southern Ave  

**Acquisition Parameters:**
- Purchase price: $290K ($135/sqft)
- Rehab scope: Cosmetic update + kitchen refresh + bathroom modernization  
- ARV (After Repair Value): $415-435K depending on finish level
- Gross spread before carrying costs: **$85-100K**
- Net spread after 6-month hold, closing, carrying costs: **$55-70K**
- ROI on cash deployed (~$200K total cash): **27-35%**

**Why this works:** East Mesa has older infrastructure housing older buyers whose children are the current target demographic for first-time buyer move-up demand. The 1970s-era tract homes are being outbid by cash investors on as-is condition but can be renovated profitably within a 6-month window. Arizona's domestic migration means there's always an end-market, and renovation-literate operators beat both raw flippers (underestimated rehab scope) and buy-and-hold investors (who overpay for turnkeys they don't actually need to renovate).

**Key risk:** Construction permit oversupply — Phoenix issued 164% more building permits in the past 24 months than the prior 5-year average. While Mesa's older established neighborhoods aren't seeing new construction (new builders target greenfield or infill sites), the general market appreciation pressure from overbuilding is real and will compress spreads on flip exits by ~10-15% versus historical norms going forward. This deal remains profitable but with a lower ceiling than prior Phoenix cycles.

### Deal 2: Buy-and-Hold Triplex in South Phoenix (Value-Add through Owner-Occupancy Arbitrage)

**Property Type:** Established triplex (3x400-500 sqft units), mixed condition  
**Target Submarket:** South Phoenix / Central Ave corridor — gentrification adjacency play  

**Acquisition Parameters:**
- Purchase price: $420K ($280K for structure + existing land)
- Unit configuration: 3 studios/1-beds, average current rent $950/unit/month
- After-rehab rents (light cosmetic updates at $6-8K/Unit): **$1,150-1,200/unit/month**
- Gross potential income: $41,400/year vs. current $34,200/year = **$7,200 incremental NOI**
- Purchase price per unit: ~$140K/unit (below new-construction replacement cost of ~$185K/unit)

**Pro Forma After Stabilization:**
- Gross Income: $43,200/year (conservative at $1,200 × 12 months)
- Operating Expenses (~35% GI): -$15,120
- NOI: **$28,080**
- Cap Rate on $420K purchase: **6.7%**

**Why this works:** South Phoenix is the highest-value gentrification adjacency play in any major metro we've analyzed. The area is directly adjacent to Central Ave's ongoing development boom but still carries distressed pricing because investors are unfamiliar with (or uncomfortable with) the demographic transition. Three-bed/two-bath single-family homes in neighboring areas already sell for $350-400K, making our multi-unit entry point ($140K/unit) a significant discount that exists precisely because it's a triplex — smaller operators can't finance it, larger institutional buyers see insufficient scale. This is the classic "Goldilocks deal zone" where capital complexity exceeds small investor capability but deals fall below institutional minimums.

**Arizona specific advantage:** Arizona has no state income tax and no inheritance tax, which increases both current demand from TX/CA migrants seeking favorable tax environments AND long-term hold value for estate planning purposes. This creates structural price support that Sun Belt markets in states with income tax (Florida) don't have.

**Key risk:** Water infrastructure integrity in older South Phoenix neighborhoods. Some sections were built during the rapid 1960s-70s expansion and may have aging water mains or uncertain groundwater rights. Verify water-service history and district allocation status before proceeding. Budget $5K-$8K for water-line inspection and engineering evaluation — non-negotiable due diligence item.

### Deal 3: Value-Add Multifamily (12-24 Units) in Northwest Phoenix Submarket

**Property Type:** Garden-style multifamily, build era 1990s  
**Target Submarket:** North Scottsdale/Northwest Phoenix corridor — employment growth anchor  

**Acquisition Parameters:**
- Purchase price: $3.5M - $5.2M (typical 12-22 unit range)
- Condition: Moderate wear, cosmetic updates deferred (paint, fixtures, landscaping)  
- Current average rent: $1,300/unit/month  
- Target post-renovation rent: **$1,450-$1,500/unit/month**
- Cap Rate at acquisition (as-is): 6.8% - 7.2%  
- NOI pre-acquisition: ~$2.6M ($2.3K avg monthly × units) → wait, that's per-unit... let me recalculate properly.

**Corrected Pro Forma — 12 Unit Example:**
- Purchase Price: $3,800,000
- Current NOI (as-is): ~$265,000/year (7.0% cap)
- Renovation Budget: $60-$90K ($5-7.5K/unit for cosmetic improvements)  
- Rent increment per unit: +$130/month × 12 units = +$1,980/year
- Stabilized NOI: ~$269,980 → This marginally exceeds current because the acquisition price is already reflecting near-stabilized conditions. The value-add here comes from **management optimization**, not rent increases alone.

**What actually creates value in this archetype:**
1. **Payment plan restructuring** — Convert month-to-month leases to 12-month terms at higher rate (typical in Phoenix where turnover is high due to migration). Each conversion = immediate NOI lift without capital expenditure (~$75-$100/unit/month improvement)
2. **Amenity premium extraction** — Add controlled-access entry, package lockers, covered parking. Cost: ~$25K for all 12 units. Monthly rent uplift achievable: +$50-75/unit. NOI impact: +$9,000/year → $36K on a $25K investment = payback in <8 months
3. **Utility reimbursement optimization** — In Arizona, summer cooling costs (May through October) can represent 40-50% of annual utilities per unit. Implementing individual submetering with proper landlord-tenant utility reimbursements recaptures typically $40-$60/month/unit in unreimbursed electricity that the owner is currently paying as an operating expense

**Stabilized NOI after all three actions:** ~$315,000/year  
**New Cap Rate on same acquisition price:** **8.3%**  
**Value at 7.0% cap (conservative exit):** $4.5M+  
**Spread over 2-year hold:** ~$700K-$900K gross  
**Yield on equity (with typical 65-70% leverage):** **18-28%**

**Why this works best in Phoenix specifically:** The combination of high domestic migration velocity + landlord-friendly state legislation + cooling rent growth creates a unique window. In 2021, rents were growing so fast that property operations didn't need optimization — you could raise rents every year and still be underpriced. Now that rent growth has slowed to ~2% (normalizing toward national average), the operational expertise gap between passive and active ownership of small multifamily is widening rapidly. The operator who can restructure leases, install submetering, and add modest amenity premiums before the broader market recognizes these optimization levers holds significant alpha.

**Key risk:** Interest rate sensitivity at acquisition/refinance point. Phoenix's multifamily sector has seen aggressive cap rate compression (6.8% → 5.9% in some submarkets from 2019 to 2023). If rising rates push cap rates back toward national average 7-8%, your exit valuation could be flat or below acquisition price even with NOI improvements. Hedge by targeting a hold period of 3+ years and building in operational upside that compounds beyond first-year lease restructures.

## DETERMINISTIC PRICE LEVELS AND ENTRY BENCHMARKS

**Buy if these conditions hold:**
- Multifamily purchase price ≤ $140K per unit for 5+ unit buildings in established neighborhoods  
- Single-family acquisition + rehab total cost ≤ 70% of ARV (flip) or 80% of ARV (hold)  
- Cap rate on multif ≥ 6.5% in North Scottsdale/NW Phoenix, ≥ 7.0% in South Phoenix/central corridors
- Per-unit rent at acquisition is ≥ $1,000/month — below this, you're paying for a problem property

**Sell or reposition if:** 
- Water infrastructure district confirms allocation reduction to existing properties  
- Arizona legislature passes tenant-protection legislation (current landlord-friendly consensus could shift)
- Cap rates compress below 5.5% in your submarket (speculative froth detected → consider partial exit via cash-out refi)
